There are two phases of love: passionate love and companionate love.

## Passionate Love

*Based on a chapter in* Modern Love *by Aziz Ansari*

Brain activity is similar to how the brain reacts to cocaine. Anxiety-related parts of the brain are also active.

<IconList icon="clock">
- Lasts around 12 to 18 months
- Spikes quickly
- Also known as the "honeymoon phase"
</IconList>

**Characteristics:**

<IconList icon="spark">
- Idealizing the partner
- Constant thoughts about the partner
- Desire to tell and know more
- Strong feelings like never before
- Craving for physical intimacy
</IconList>


## Companionate Love

Companionate love is very similar to the love you share with family members or an old friend.

<IconList icon="clock">
- Takes time to build
- Lasts longer
- Feels Mundane and ordinary
</IconList>

**Characteristics:**

<IconList icon="leaf">
- Unshakable trust
- Deep intimacy
- Long-term commitment
- Genuine affection despite knowing each other's flaws
</IconList>

Passionate love spikes early and fades into a low hum; companionate love builds slowly over decades into a steady, higher plateau.
</Figure>

<Aside label="Related experiment">
*Neural correlates of long-term intense romantic love*

**Process:** Participants who had been married for an average of 21.4 years and reported still being in love were shown facial photographs of their spouse while their brain activity was measured (fMRI). This was compared with a study of early-stage couples (1 to 17 months).

**Observations:** In early-stage couples, brain activity spiked cortisol and dopamine. In late-stage couples, it triggered opioid circuits related to safety and calm.
</Aside>

## Danger Points When Transitioning from Passionate to Companionate Love

<Figure src="/posts/15112026_twophasesoflove/danger-points.svg" alt="A chart over time showing passionate love spiking quickly then crashing into a fading oscillation, while companionate love rises slowly and steadily; two danger points are marked where passionate love peaks and where it first crashes.">
Passionate love spikes and crashes; companionate love builds slowly. The danger points sit right at the peak, and right after the first crash.
</Figure>

*Based on* The Happiness Hypothesis

1. **Peak of passionate love** — at this phase, people tend to make drastic decisions, like proposing marriage or making unrealistic promises. This leads you to make decisions you would otherwise not make in a sane state of mind.
2. **When passionate love is wearing off** — at this phase, the obsessive high fades and people mistake it for a lack of chemistry. If a couple relied purely on physical attraction and novelty, they realize once the high fades that they don't have shared values or a deep friendship. Many relationships break at this point.

If you can avoid these two danger points, you may transition into a deeper and more meaningful phase of the relationship: companionate love.

## Learnings

1. Delay big decisions and major commitments early on. Be a bit practical at the start.
2. Invest in friendship early. Build shared goals, values, and respect, so when the high fades you still have something to hold on to.
3. Understand that novelty fades after a certain time. Knowing that this drug will fade prepares you for something real. The thing that was so interesting at the start won't be as novel after a year.
